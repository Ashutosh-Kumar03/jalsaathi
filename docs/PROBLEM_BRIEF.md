# JalSaathi: Problem Brief

**Team:** Code Hustlers | **Hack With Delhi 4.0**
**Track 7: Climate, Energy and Resource Efficiency. Problem: Monitoring and Reducing Agricultural Water Footprint.**

## 1. The problem

Groundwater in north-west India is being drawn faster than it is replenished. In the 2025 national assessment,
Punjab's stage of groundwater extraction was 156.36 percent, Rajasthan's 147.11 percent and Haryana's
136.75 percent. Nationally, 730 of 6,762 assessment units (10.8 percent) are classified as over-exploited.
In Punjab, irrigation accounted for 24.95 BCM of the 26.32 BCM of groundwater extracted in 2025-26.
Groundwater levels in Punjab are reported to be falling by about 41.6 cm per year.

## 2. Who is affected

Small and marginal farmers growing wheat and rice in groundwater-stressed districts. They pay for diesel or
electricity to pump water, and they lose the most when the water table falls and borewells must go deeper.

## 3. What farmers do today

Most irrigate by habit: a fixed gap in days, or a permanently flooded paddy field, without knowing that week's
actual crop water need or whether rain is coming.

## 4. Why existing solutions fall short

- Water-saving practices exist, for example alternate wetting and drying (AWD). Research on Punjab reports that
  AWD and direct-seeded rice can cut irrigation water use by 20 to 40 percent without hurting yields. World Bank
  cited demo plots found 6 to 25 percent water savings in paddy with AWD and plotting.
- Sensor-based tools exist too. The PRANA project introduced AWD pipes and digital sensors so that farmers irrigate
  only when required. These depend on hardware that has to be bought, installed and maintained, so reach stays
  limited to the plots where they are installed.
- The gap: a **zero-hardware, day-by-day, field-specific answer** in the farmer's own language.

## 5. Our solution

JalSaathi tells a farmer every morning whether to water today, how much, and why, using real weather data for the
exact field location. It supports wheat and rice, works in Hindi and English, and needs no sensor and no login.
It also reads the advice aloud, keeps working offline with the last saved weather, and shows estimated savings
against the farmer's own habit in litres, pump hours and rupees.

## 6. Value and adoption

The cost to the farmer is zero. Adoption path: (1) pilot with a Krishi Vigyan Kendra and one Farmer Producer
Organisation, whose extension workers can onboard farmers a village at a time; (2) validate the advice against
PAU or HAU recommendations and farmer-recorded irrigation; (3) add SMS or WhatsApp alerts and more crops.
Impact is measured as litres of water and pumping hours saved per acre against the farmer's own baseline,
to be verified in the pilot.

## 7. Honest limits

All numbers are estimates from a simplified FAO-56 water balance and have not been validated in field trials.
Crop and soil parameters use standard ranges. Savings depend on the habit, pump hours and cost the farmer types in.
Alerts are in-app and spoken; SMS is not built yet because it needs a paid gateway.
